    How Chunk Keeper works

    The idea

    Minecraft only generates a chunk once. Chunk Keeper writes a copy of your world that contains only the chunks you keep; every other chunk is missing, so Minecraft generates it again with its current world generation (new biomes, new structures) the next time a player gets close. Same seed, same terrain shape; only what the new version changed will differ.

    Colors

    • Keep – copied exactly as it is.
    • Suggested – looks like your work (player-placed blocks, storage, pets and villagers you traded with, portals, where players are).
    • Will regenerate – shown with "Regenerate preview".
    • Unfinished edge chunk – at the border of explored land; regenerating these is recommended.

    Mouse and keys

    • Left drag use the tool · Right drag or Space+drag move · Wheel zoom
    • B keep brush · E remove brush · K keep box · X remove box · I inspect · H move
    • [ ] brush size · Ctrl+Z undo · Ctrl+Y redo · F fit · G grid · S suggestions · P preview · T time spent

    Borders between kept and regenerated land

    If the Minecraft version you open the copy with considers your kept chunks "old", it blends the new terrain into them: the old biome continues roughly 5 chunks into the regenerated area and the terrain there is smoothed. A version that changed world generation marks every older chunk this way when it loads it, so expect that ring after such an upgrade. Remove old-chunk marks only clears marks the chunks already carry. Water at the edge of kept chunks can flow into the new terrain, and the review lists those spots.

    Safety

    Chunk Keeper only reads your world. Selections and the map cache are stored in your ChunkKeeper folder, not in the world. Close Minecraft before applying, and keep a backup of worlds you care about.
